# Dental consent content

Use on leaflets, web pages, videos and scripts about a dental or oral surgery procedure. This skill checks **what** the material says. The other skills check **how** it says it (`plain-language-rewrite`, `risk-communication`, `yasashii-nihongo`).

Sources (see docs/guidelines.md, "Dentistry"):
- RCS England, Faculty of Dental Surgery. *Parameters of care for patients undergoing mandibular third molar surgery* (2020).
- Australian Dental Association. *Guidelines for Consent for Care in Dentistry* (2020).
- 日本歯科麻酔学会ほか. 歯科治療による下歯槽神経・舌神経損傷の診断とその治療に関するガイドライン (2019).
- Lewis MA, Newton JT. *Br Dent J* 2006;201:114-117 (quality of dental leaflets).
- Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board [2015] UKSC 11: material risks are those a reasonable person in the patient's position would want to know, or this patient would.

## Step 1: ask the author

- Which procedure, and for whom (all patients, or a sub-group such as "tooth close to the nerve")?
- Is the material a **consent aid** (helps a patient decide) or **information only** (for example a teaching or research material on one risk)? Information-only material may leave items out on purpose: record which ones and why, so it can be stated as a limitation.

## Step 2: checklist

Mark each item Present / Partly / Missing / Left out on purpose (with the reason).

| # | Item | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The aim of the material is stated first (what it is for, who it is for) | Lewis & Newton |
| 2 | What the procedure is and why it may be offered (the benefit or reason) | ADA; RCS |
| 3 | **Material risks with numbers**, each with who it applies to and the time frame. For lower wisdom teeth at least: nerve injury to lip and chin (inferior alveolar nerve), tongue nerve (lingual nerve), temporary vs permanent, dry socket, infection, bleeding, swelling and limited mouth opening | RCS; ADA (names "nerve damage in oral surgery"); JP 2019 |
| 4 | What the risk feels like and what to do if it happens (symptoms, who to contact) | RCS; JP 2019 |
| 5 | **Alternatives**, including monitoring and no treatment, and where relevant coronectomy for a tooth close to the nerve | RCS; ADA; Lewis & Newton |
| 6 | **Risks of waiting or declining** (for example infection, decay of the next tooth) | RCS |
| 7 | **Factors that change the risk** (for lower wisdom teeth: age, how close the roots are to the nerve on imaging, how deep the tooth is) | RCS |
| 8 | Who does the procedure and when referral to a specialist is usual. Do not claim a specialist lowers the risk unless the cited evidence shows it | ADA (scope); evidence check |
| 9 | A next step: talk it through with the dentist or surgeon, and how to ask questions. Written information alone is not enough for consent | ADA |
| 10 | Sources, date written, review date, and who wrote it | Lewis & Newton; PIF TICK |

## Step 3: output

1. The table above, filled in.
2. For each Missing item: a suggested sentence in plain language, with a source for every number (run it past `risk-communication`).
3. A one-paragraph scope note listing the items left out on purpose, ready for the methods or limitations section.

Do not add content the author has ruled out. Suggest it, and leave the decision to them.
